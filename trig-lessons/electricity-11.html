The electric generator: from motion to current

Almost all the electricity in your home was made by spinning a coil inside a magnet. Go through the 7 short steps, then crank your own generator in the lab.

Step 1 of 7
  1. 1
    The problem

    Why does a bicycle lamp get brighter when you pedal faster?

    Some bicycles have a lamp with no battery. A small dynamo touches the wheel; pedal and the lamp lights, pedal faster and it gets brighter. Motion goes in, electricity comes out: the opposite of a motor.

    Some bicycles have a front lamp with no battery. A small device called a dynamo rubs against the wheel.

    Stand still: the lamp is dark. Pedal: it lights. Pedal faster: it gets brighter.

    In Level 10, a motor turned electricity into motion. Can we run it backwards, and turn motion into electricity?

    Where does the energy come from?

    From your legs. Pedalling with the dynamo on feels a little harder, because some of your kinetic energy is being turned into electrical energy for the lamp.

  2. 2
    The key experiment

    Run a motor backwards

    The book's activity: connect a toy motor with a small fan to a battery, and the fan spins. Now connect the motor to an LED instead and spin the fan with your fingers: the LED lights, and it gets brighter the faster you spin.

    The book's activity: a small toy motor with a fan fixed to it, an LED (or a small bulb), a battery and wires. Try both steps with the picture:

    1. Connect the motor to the battery. Observation: the fan spins. The motor converts electrical energy into kinetic energy.
    2. Connect the motor to the LED instead, and spin the fan with your fingers, slowly, then faster. Observation: the LED lights, and gets brighter the faster you spin. Mechanical (kinetic) energy is converted into electrical energy.

    Book question: the LED doesn't light

    Why does an undamaged LED sometimes not light when it's connected to a battery? Because the current flows in the opposite direction to the one it's supposed to. To light it, reverse its connection to the battery.

  3. 3
    The generator

    Spin a coil in a magnet, get electricity

    An electric generator (dynamo) converts mechanical (kinetic) energy into electrical energy. Its principle: when its coil is rotated between the poles of a magnet, an electromotive force is produced, which makes a current flow.
    Your book's definition

    Electric generator (dynamo) المولد الكهربي (الدينامو): a device that converts mechanical (kinetic) energy into electrical energy.

    It has the same parts as a motor: a coil, a magnet, and brushes touching rings or a commutator on the coil's ends.

    Its principle (book): when the coil is rotated between the poles of the magnet, an electromotive force is produced, which makes an electric current flow.

    Why does spinning make a current?

    As the coil turns, the amount of magnetic field passing through it keeps changing. A changing magnetic field through a coil pushes charges round it. No change, no push: a coil sitting still in a magnet makes nothing.

  4. 4
    More electricity

    Spin faster, or use a stronger magnet

    A generator makes more current if you increase the coil's rotational speed or the magnetic field's strength. That's why the bicycle lamp gets brighter as you pedal faster.

    The current a generator makes increases by increasing:

    1The rotational speed of the coil
    2The strength of the magnetic field
    • Give reason: a bicycle dynamo's lamp gets brighter as the wheel turns faster. Because the electromotive force produced by the dynamo increases as the rotational speed of its coil increases.
    • What happens if the dynamo's coil spins faster? The emf and the current increase, so the lamp gets brighter.

    Compare with the motor

    In a motor, more current gives more turning. In a generator it's the other way round: more turning gives more current.

  5. 5
    Two kinds of current

    Direct current and alternating current

    Direct current (DC) has a constant value and flows in one direction, like a battery's. Alternating current (AC) changes its value and direction with time. A DC generator uses a commutator; power stations use AC generators.
    Your book's definitions

    Direct current (DC) التيار المستمر: an electric current that has a constant intensity and flows in one direction in the circuit.

    Alternating current (AC) التيار المتردد: an electric current whose value and direction change with time.

    What does the commutator do here?

    Every half turn, the current in a spinning coil wants to reverse. The commutator swaps the connections at that moment, so the current outside always flows the same way.

  6. 6
    Generators in real life

    Power stations, emergency generators and dynamos

    Power stations turn huge generator coils with turbines, driven by falling water, wind or steam, to make electricity for cities and factories. Emergency generators and bicycle dynamos are smaller generators.
    • Power stations: huge turbines turn the coils of large generators. The turbines are driven by falling water (waterfalls and dams), wind, or steam.
    • Give reason: huge turbines are used to drive the coils of large generators in power stations. To produce the electricity needed by cities and factories.
    • Emergency generators: an engine turns the coil when the mains power is cut (hospitals, buildings).
    • Bicycle dynamo: the wheel turns the coil.

    Egypt's High Dam

    At the High Dam in Aswan, water falling through the dam turns turbines, which turn generators. It's the same idea as spinning the toy fan with your fingers, just enormous.

  7. 7
    Putting it together

    Motor or generator? Same parts, opposite jobs

    A motor takes in electrical energy and gives out kinetic energy; a generator does the opposite. The same device can be both: connect its brushes to a battery and it's a motor; connect them to a lamp and spin the coil, and it's a generator.

    Book question: how can one coil-and-magnet device be used

    • as a motor? Connect the two graphite brushes to a battery: a current flows through the coil, a torque is produced, and the coil rotates.
    • as a generator to light a lamp? Connect the two brushes to a lamp and rotate the coil: an emf is produced, a current flows and the lamp lights.

    Back to the bicycle: the wheel spins the dynamo's coil inside a magnet, so an emf is made and the lamp lights. Pedal faster, the coil spins faster, the emf is bigger: a brighter lamp.
