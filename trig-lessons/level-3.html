Find a missing angle

In Level 2 you knew an angle and found a side. Now you'll do the opposite: use two sides to find the angle. Go through the 7 short steps one at a time. Each step ends with a quick question.

Step 1 of 7
  1. 1
    Why learn this?

    Sometimes you know the sides, but need the angle

    Lengths are easy to measure with a tape measure. Angles are harder, especially on big things. But often the angle is what really matters:

    • Ladders. Too steep and the ladder can fall backwards. Too flat and the bottom slides out. The safe angle is about 75°.
    • Wheelchair ramps. Too steep and they're too hard to push up. A ramp should be about 5° or less.
    • Roofs. A roof must be steep enough for rain to run off.

    You can't hold a protractor up to a big ladder. But you can measure two lengths. This level shows you how to turn two lengths into the angle.

  2. 2
    The big idea

    Go backwards: from the ratio to the angle

    Until now you went one way: start with an angle, get its ratio. For example, sin 30° = 0.5.

    Now you go backwards: start with the ratio, find the angle. Which angle has a sin of 0.5? The answer is 30°.

    It's like the contacts on a phone. Normally you look up a name to find the number. Going backwards, you see a number and ask: whose number is this?

    You can even read the Level 1 table backwards: find 0.50 in the sin column, then read the angle in that row. It's 30°.

  3. 3
    The backwards buttons

    Meet sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹

    Your calculator has a backwards button for each one. You say them "inverse sine", "inverse cos" and "inverse tan".

    To use one, press SHIFT (on some calculators it's called 2nd), then sin. Then type the ratio.

    SHIFT+sin=sin⁻¹
    sin⁻¹

    Put in opposite ÷ hypotenuse. Get the angle.

    Example: sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°

    cos⁻¹

    Put in adjacent ÷ hypotenuse. Get the angle.

    Example: cos⁻¹(0.5) = 60°

    tan⁻¹

    Put in opposite ÷ adjacent. Get the angle.

    Example: tan⁻¹(1) = 45°

    Careful

    The little −1 is not a power

    sin⁻¹ does not mean 1 ÷ sin. The −1 is just a label that means "backwards".

    In and out

    A ratio goes in, an angle comes out

    What goes in is a ratio: just a number, with no unit (like 0.5; why no unit?). What comes out is an angle, in degrees (like 30°).

  4. 4
    Why it works

    Each ratio belongs to just one angle

    Remember Level 1: same angle, same shape, same ratio. So every angle has its own sin.

    Look at the graph. As the angle gets bigger, its sin gets bigger too. It never goes back down, from 0° all the way to 90°.

    So each sin value belongs to just one angle. That's why going backwards gives one clear answer.

    To go backwards on the graph: start at 0.5 on the left, go across to the curve, then straight down. You land on 30°.

  5. 5
    How to find an angle

    Four steps

    1. Name the sides from the angle you want (θ). How?
    2. Pick sin, cos or tan: the one that uses the two sides you know (SOH CAH TOA).
    3. Divide to get the ratio.
    4. Go backwards: press SHIFT, then sin, cos or tan, then type the ratio.
    Example: is the ladder safe?

    A 4 m ladder's foot is 1 m from the wall. What angle does it make with the ground?

    1. The ladder is the hypotenuse. The 1 m on the ground touches θ, so it's adjacent.
    2. Adjacent and hypotenuse: CAH, so use cos.
    3. cos θ = 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25
    4. θ = cos⁻¹(0.25) ≈ 75.5°. About 75°, so it's safe!
    Example: is the ramp OK?

    A ramp rises 0.5 m over 6 m along the ground. What is its angle?

    1. The rise is opposite θ. The 6 m along the ground is adjacent.
    2. Opposite and adjacent: TOA, so use tan.
    3. tan θ = 0.5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.083
    4. θ = tan⁻¹(0.083) ≈ 4.8°. Less than 5°, so it's OK for a wheelchair!
  6. 6
    Check your answer

    Does it make sense?

    • Guess first. If the opposite is shorter than the adjacent, the angle is less than 45°. If it's longer, the angle is more than 45°.
    • sin and cos are never more than 1. The hypotenuse is the longest side, so opposite ÷ hypotenuse is always less than 1. If you get more than 1, and the calculator says "Math ERROR", you divided the wrong way round.
    • The answer must be between 0° and 90°.
    • Check the calculator is in degrees (DEG). Test it: sin⁻¹(0.5) should give 30.
  7. 7
    Real life

    Who finds angles?

    Back to the ladder from step 1: 75.5° is about 75°, so it's safe. Builders even have a quick rule for this: for every 4 m of ladder, put the foot 1 m out from the wall. Now you know why that rule works: cos⁻¹(1 ÷ 4) ≈ 75°.

    • Ramp designers

      Ramps often rise 1 m for every 12 m along the ground. tan⁻¹(1 ÷ 12) ≈ 4.8°, gentle enough for a wheelchair.

    • Builders

      They check a roof is steep enough for rain and snow to slide off.

    • Pilots

      Planes come down to land on a gentle slope of about 3°.

    • Phones

      A spirit-level app measures how much your phone is tilted, using the same backwards maths.
