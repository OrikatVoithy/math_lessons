Power, energy and the electricity bill

Why does a heater cost more to run than a lamp? How is the electricity bill worked out? Go through the 7 short steps, then plan your own home's bill in the lab.

Step 1 of 7
  1. 1
    The problem

    Two lamps give the same light. Which is cheaper to run?

    An LED lamp (12 W) and a CFL lamp (20 W) give about the same light. Lit for 12 hours a day, which costs less each month, and how much less? The answer needs power, energy and the price of electricity.

    In the shop, two lamps give about the same light: an LED lamp marked 12 W, and a compact fluorescent (CFL) lamp marked 20 W.

    Your family lights it for 12 hours every day. Over a month, which one costs less on the electricity bill, and by how many pounds?

    To answer, we need three ideas: power (what the W means), energy, and how the bill is calculated.

    Why it matters

    Every home in Egypt pays for electricity by the kilowatt-hour. Choosing the right appliances and using them well saves money and protects the environment.

  2. 2
    Power

    Power is how much energy is used each second

    Electric power (P) is the electrical energy an appliance uses each second: P = E ÷ t. It's measured in watts: 1 W = 1 J per second. A 9 W lamp uses 9 J every second.
    Your book's definition

    Electric power (P) القدرة الكهربية of an appliance: the amount of electrical energy (E) it consumes per second. It expresses the rate at which an appliance uses energy.

    P = Etpower = energy ÷ time

    Energy is in joules (J), time in seconds (s), so power is in watts (W): 1 W = 1 J/s. "An LED lamp of 9 W" means it uses 9 J of electrical energy every second.

    The energy label: new appliances carry a label from A to E, so you can choose the most efficient one:

    • AGreen: the highest efficiency, the lowest energy use
    • BBlue: in between
    • CYellow: in between
    • DOrange: in between
    • ERed: the lowest efficiency, the highest energy use

    How your book defines the watt

    "The watt: the power of an electrical appliance that consumes an electric energy of one joule in one second."

    "What is meant by…?" with numbers

    "An electric torch consumes 12,000 J per minute." P = E ÷ t = 12,000 ÷ 60 = 200 W.

    Why the energy label matters

    It lets buyers choose the product with the highest efficiency and lowest energy use, and protects them from products that waste energy.

  3. 3
    Turn it around

    Finding the energy or the time

    Triangle with E on top: P = E ÷ t, E = P × t, t = E ÷ P. Time in seconds when using joules and watts.

    The same cover-up triangle again, with E on top and P and t underneath.

    Find P (book)

    An appliance uses 9 × 10⁵ J in 10 minutes. What is its power?

    1. t = 10 × 60 = 600 s.
    2. P = 900,000 ÷ 600 = 1500 W.
    Find E (book)

    How much energy, in kJ, does an 88 W fan use in one minute?

    1. E = P × t = 88 × 60 = 5280 J.
    2. In kJ: 5280 ÷ 1000 = 5.28 kJ.
    Find t (book)

    A 100 W bulb is lit for 5 hours. How long must a 250 W bulb be lit to use the same energy?

    1. First bulb: E = 100 × (5 × 3600) = 1,800,000 J.
    2. Second bulb: t = E ÷ P = 1,800,000 ÷ 250 = 7200 s = 2 hours.

    Joule and watt-second

    Since E = P × t, a joule is the same as a watt × second. Your book asks for this: "the unit of energy equivalent to watt.second" is the joule.

  4. 4
    What happens if…?

    More energy each second means more power

    At constant time, power and energy are directly related. At constant energy, power and time are inversely related: a more powerful appliance uses the same energy in less time.
    If…Example (start: 200 J in 20 s = 10 W)Power
    Energy doubles, same time400 ÷ 20 = 20 W× 2
    Time doubles, same energy200 ÷ 40 = 5 W÷ 2
    Energy doubles and time halves400 ÷ 10 = 40 W× 4

    Your book's words: power and energy have a direct relationship (at constant time). Power and time have an inverse relationship (at constant energy): the higher the power, the less time it takes to use a given amount of energy.

  5. 5
    A bigger unit

    The kilowatt-hour

    The joule is too small for real life, so electricity is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). 1 kW = 1000 W, 1 h = 3600 s, so 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

    Give reason: the kWh is used instead of the joule in practical life. Because the joule is a very small unit of electrical energy. A kettle uses about 90,000 J just to boil a cup of water.

    1 kW = 1000 W, and 1 h = 60 × 60 = 3600 s. So 1 kWh = 1000 × 3600 = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

    • From kWh to J: multiply by 3.6 × 10⁶.
    • From J to kWh: divide by 3.6 × 10⁶.
    • Energy in kWh = power in kW × time in hours.
    Book example

    A 20 W bulb is lit for 5 hours. How much energy does it use, in kWh?

    1. Power in kW: 20 ÷ 1000 = 0.02 kW.
    2. E = 0.02 × 5 = 0.1 kWh.

    The meter at home

    The electricity meter in your home counts kilowatt-hours. Each "unit" on the bill is one kWh.

    A kWh isn't a unit of power

    kW is power (how fast energy is used). kWh is energy (how much was used in total). A 1 kW heater running for 1 hour uses 1 kWh.

  6. 6
    The bill

    Working out what electricity costs

    1) Watts ÷ 1000 = kW. 2) Count the hours. 3) kWh = kW × hours. 4) Cost = kWh × price per kWh (in Egyptian pounds).
    1. Power in kilowatts: kW = W ÷ 1000.
    2. The number of operating hours.
    3. Energy used: kWh = kW × hours.
    4. Cost: pounds = kWh × price per kWh.
    Book example: the kettle

    A family uses a 1500 W kettle for half an hour a day. The price is 1.55 pounds per kWh. What does it cost per week?

    1. 1500 ÷ 1000 = 1.5 kW.
    2. 0.5 h × 7 days = 3.5 h.
    3. 1.5 × 3.5 = 5.25 kWh.
    4. 5.25 × 1.55 ≈ 8.14 pounds a week.

    Typical powers (book table):

    Laptop50 WElectric heater1–2 kW
    Television100 WClothes iron1–2.5 kW
    Fan50–100 WAir conditioner1.5–2.5 kW

    Air conditioners and things that heat (irons, heaters, kettles) use the most electricity.

    Choosing an appliance (book)

    Four vacuum cleaners clean equally well: 2000 W, 2400 W, 1800 W and 1700 W. Choose the 1700 W one: its power is the lowest, so it uses the least energy and costs the least to run.

    The rule

    "The lower the power rating of a lamp or an appliance, the lower its electricity consumption, and so the lower its cost."

  7. 7
    Back to the lamps

    Now we can solve step 1, and stay safe

    LED: 0.012 kW × 360 h = 4.32 kWh ≈ 6.7 pounds a month. CFL: 0.02 kW × 360 h = 7.2 kWh ≈ 11.2 pounds. The LED saves about 4.5 pounds a month for the same light. Plus five safety rules.
    LED (12 W)CFL (20 W)
    Power in kW0.012 kW0.02 kW
    Hours a month12 h × 30 days = 360 h
    Energy a month0.012 × 360 = 4.32 kWh0.02 × 360 = 7.2 kWh
    Cost (1.55 pounds/kWh)≈ 6.7 pounds≈ 11.2 pounds

    Give reason: LED lamps are preferred to CFL lamps of the same light. Because they use less electrical energy for the same time, so they're more efficient and save energy and money, protecting financial and environmental resources.

    Safety with household electricity (book)

    1. Never use household electricity for school experiments.
    2. Don't touch wires or circuit parts with wet hands. G.R.: because tap water conducts electricity.
    3. Make sure wires are properly insulated. G.R.: to avoid electric shock.
    4. Don't connect several high-power appliances to one socket. G.R.: to avoid a large increase in current, which may cause fires.
    5. Disconnect the source before connecting or replacing any part of a circuit.

    Linking back

    Overloading a socket is Level 7 again: every appliance is a parallel branch, so each one adds to the total current through the socket's wires. Too much current makes them hot (Level 4).
