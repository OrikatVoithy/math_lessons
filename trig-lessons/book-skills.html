Trigonometry · Book skills (between Levels 2 and 3)

School book skills

Your school book asks about the same ideas you've learned, but in its own way: with letters, Pythagoras, exact fractions and proofs. Learn its language here, so your tests feel familiar. Go through the 7 short steps one at a time.

Step 1 of 7
  1. 1
    Why learn this?

    Same ideas, different language

    A question in your book might look like this:

    In △XYZ, m(∠Z) = 90°, XZ = 9 cm and YZ = 12 cm. Find sin X, cos X and tan X.

    It looks hard, but it only uses things you already know. Here's what it means:

    △XYZ
    a triangle with corners called X, Y and Z
    m(∠Z) = 90°
    the angle at corner Z is a right angle (the square corner)
    XZ = 9 cm
    the side that joins X and Z is 9 cm long
    Find sin X
    use the angle at corner X as θ

    The book also wants you to find the third side first (with Pythagoras), and to write answers as exact fractions. This level teaches all of that, so you can do the questions in your book.

  2. 2
    Names with letters

    Reading △ABC

    • Corners get capital letters: A, B, C.
    • An angle is named by its corner: ∠A. Sometimes by three letters with the corner in the middle: ∠BAC is the angle at A.
    • m(∠B) = 90° means "the measure of the angle at B is 90°": the right angle is at B.
    • A side is named by the two corners it joins: AC joins A and C.
    The letter trick

    The side opposite an angle uses the other two letters. Opposite ∠A is BC, because it doesn't touch A.

    The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. If m(∠B) = 90°, the hypotenuse is AC.

    The adjacent side of ∠A is the other side that touches A: AB.

  3. 3
    Pythagoras first

    Find the third side before the ratios

    Book questions usually give you only two sides. To write all the ratios, you need the third. Use Pythagoras:

    short side² + short side² = hypotenuse²

    • Missing hypotenuse: add the squares. XZ = 9, YZ = 12: 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, so XY = √225 = 15 cm.
    • Missing short side: take away. Hypotenuse 13, one side 5: 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so the other side is √144 = 12.

    Then the question from step 1 is easy: from ∠X, the opposite side is YZ = 12, the adjacent side is XZ = 9, and the hypotenuse is XY = 15. So sin X = 12/15 = 4/5.

    A shortcut: number families

    Some sets of whole numbers fit Pythagoras exactly. Book questions use them a lot, so if you spot two of them, you know the third:

    3, 4, 55, 12, 138, 15, 177, 24, 25

    Any multiple works too: 6, 8, 10 and 9, 12, 15 are 3, 4, 5 made bigger (same shape).

  4. 4
    Exact answers

    Fractions and roots, not decimals

    Your book wants exact answers: write sin X = 4/5, not 0.8. (Why exact is better.)

    Simplify fractions

    Divide the top and bottom by the same number until you can't any more.

    12/15: both divide by 3, so it's 4/5.

    10/26: both divide by 2, so it's 5/13.

    Simplify roots

    Look for a square number (4, 9, 16, 25…) hidden inside.

    √8 = √(4 × 2) = 2√2, because √4 = 2.

    √12 = √(4 × 3) = 2√3 and √18 = √(9 × 2) = 3√2.

    Decimals are still right for real-life measuring (like the building heights in Level 5). For book questions, give the fraction or the root.

  5. 5
    From one ratio

    Draw a triangle that fits the ratio

    Sometimes the book gives you one ratio and asks for the others: "If sin θ = 1/3, find cos θ and tan θ."

    The trick: draw a triangle that fits. sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = 1/3, so make the opposite side 1 and the hypotenuse 3.

    Is that allowed? Yes. Any triangle with this angle has the same ratio (same angle, same shape), so the simplest one is fine.

    1. Pythagoras for the adjacent side: 3² − 1² = 8, so it's √8 = 2√2.
    2. cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse = 2√2/3
    3. tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent = 1/(2√2)

    With a number family it's even easier: if cos β = 5/13, the sides are 5 and 13, so the third is 12. Then sin β = 12/13 and tan β = 12/5.

  6. 6
    Both angles

    The rules for the two acute angles

    In △ABC with m(∠B) = 90°, the angles A and C share the same three sides. What's opposite A is next to C, and the other way round (like sin 30° and cos 60°). That gives three rules your book uses all the time:

    • sin A = cos C and cos A = sin C: both are the same division, BC ÷ AC or AB ÷ AC.
    • tan A × tan C = 1: tan A = BC/AB and tan C = AB/BC, the same fraction upside down.
    • sin²A + cos²A = 1: this is just Pythagoras divided by AC². (sin²A means sin A × sin A.)
    How to write a proof

    Prove that tan A × tan C = 1. A proof just means writing the steps that show it's true:

    tan A = BC/AB  (opposite ÷ adjacent, from A)
    tan C = AB/BC  (opposite ÷ adjacent, from C)
    tan A × tan C = (BC/AB) × (AB/BC) = (BC × AB)/(AB × BC) = 1 ✓

    Start from the definitions, use the letters, and end with exactly what you were asked to show.

  7. 7
    Figures with a line inside

    A height makes two right triangles

    Many book figures draw a line from the top of a triangle straight down to the bottom side, meeting it at a right angle (AD ⊥ BC means "AD is at right angles to BC").

    That line splits the figure into two right triangles that share a side (AD), just like the flagpole in Level 5. Solve one triangle, then use the shared side in the other.

    Example

    AD ⊥ BC, AD = 6 cm, BD = 8 cm and DC = 4.5 cm. Find tan(∠BAD) and the length of AC.

    Triangle ABD: from ∠BAD, BD is opposite and AD is adjacent: tan(∠BAD) = 8/6 = 4/3.

    Triangle ADC: Pythagoras: AC² = 6² + 4.5² = 36 + 20.25 = 56.25, so AC = 7.5 cm.
