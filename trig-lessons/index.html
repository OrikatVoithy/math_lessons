Trigonometry · Level 1 of 6

What are sin, cos and tan?

Go through the 7 short steps one at a time. Each step ends with a quick question. After that, play with the triangle and try the practice.

Step 1 of 7
  1. 1
    The problem

    Some things are hard to measure

    How tall is this tree? You can't climb it with a tape measure.

    But two things are easy to measure from the ground:

    • How far you stand from the tree. Here it is 10 m.
    • The angle you look up to see the top. Here it is 40°.

    sin, cos and tan help you get the height from those two easy numbers. Let's see how.

  2. 2
    Meet θ

    θ is the name of the angle we choose

    The tree, the ground and your line of sight make a right triangle. That is a triangle with one square corner, like the corner of a page. The square corner is always 90°.

    A right triangle has 3 corners. One is the square corner. We pick one of the other two to work with.

    We call the angle we picked θ. It's a Greek letter, said "THAY-ta". It's just a name, like calling a number x in algebra. We use a letter because the angle is different in every problem.

    In the tree picture, θ is the 40° angle where you stand.

  3. 3
    The sides

    Name the sides from θ

    Stand at θ and look at the triangle. Each side gets a name:

    Hypotenuse الوتر
    The longest side. It is across from the square corner.
    Opposite المقابل
    The side across from θ. It doesn't touch θ.
    Adjacent المجاور
    The other side that touches θ. "Adjacent" means "next to".
  4. 4
    The big discovery

    Same angle, same shape

    Look at the picture. All three triangles have the same angle θ. The big ones are just the small one made bigger.

    Now divide the opposite by the hypotenuse in each one:

    • 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6
    • 6 ÷ 10 = 0.6
    • 9 ÷ 15 = 0.6

    Always 0.6. The size changes, but the answer stays the same. It only depends on the angle.

  5. 5
    So what are sin, cos and tan?

    Every angle has 3 special numbers

    In step 4, the opposite ÷ the hypotenuse was always 0.6 for that angle. That number has a name. It is called the sin of the angle.

    You can divide two sides in three ways. Each way has a name: sin, cos and tan. The bold lines show which two sides each one uses.

    SOH
    sin θ = oppositehypotenuse

    جا θ (sin) = المقابل ÷ الوتر

    Example: sin 30° = 0.5. So the opposite side is half as long as the hypotenuse.

    CAH
    cos θ = adjacenthypotenuse

    جتا θ (cos) = المجاور ÷ الوتر

    Example: cos 60° = 0.5. So the adjacent side is half as long as the hypotenuse.

    TOA
    tan θ = oppositeadjacent

    ظا θ (tan) = المقابل ÷ المجاور

    Example: tan 45° = 1. So the opposite and adjacent sides are the same length.

    To remember which sides go with which, say "soh-cah-toa" out loud: Sin = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse, Cos = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse, Tan = Opposite ÷ Adjacent.

    Why are they useful?

    They save you from measuring. Long ago, people drew triangles for every angle and measured the sides very carefully. They wrote the answers in big tables, like this small one:

    Anglesincostan
    10°0.170.980.18
    30°0.500.870.58
    45°0.710.711.00
    60°0.870.501.73
    80°0.980.175.67

    Today you don't need the table. Your calculator has all of it inside. Type sin 30 and it shows 0.5.

    Good question

    Do sin, cos and tan have a unit?

    No. Each one is a length divided by a length, so the units cancel out: 5 cm ÷ 10 cm = 0.5. That's 0.5, not 0.5 cm.

    You get the same 0.5 in cm, metres or inches. So they describe the shape of the triangle, not its size.

    tan is like the steepness on a road sign. tan 10° ≈ 0.18 means you go 0.18 m up for every 1 m along the ground.

    The whole idea

    Ratio × the side you know

    side you want = side you know × ratio

    The angle gives you the ratio (sin, cos or tan). The side you know sets the size.

    Ladder: height = 5 m × sin 60° = 5 m × 0.87 ≈ 4.3 m

    • Know the hypotenuse, want the opposite: × sin
    • Know the hypotenuse, want the adjacent: × cos
    • Know the adjacent, want the opposite: × tan

    If the side you want is the hypotenuse, you divide instead. That's Level 2.

  6. 6
    How to use them

    Three steps

    1. Write what you know and what you want. Usually that's one angle, one side you know, and one side you want.
    2. Pick sin, cos or tan. Choose the one that uses both of your sides (check SOH CAH TOA).
    3. Put in the numbers. Get the value from the calculator, then multiply.
    Example

    A 5 m ladder leans on a wall. It makes a 60° angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

    1. Know: θ = 60°, and the ladder is the hypotenuse = 5 m. Want: the height. That is the opposite side.
    2. Opposite and hypotenuse go together in SOH. So use sin.
    3. sin 60° = 0.87 (from the table or a calculator). So the height is 5 × 0.87 ≈ 4.3 m.
  7. 7
    Back to the tree

    Now we can solve step 1

    You stand 10 m from the tree and look up at θ = 40°.

    • The height is opposite θ. The 10 m on the ground is adjacent to θ.
    • Opposite and adjacent go together in TOA. So use tan.
    • tan 40° = 0.84. So the height is 10 × 0.84 ≈ 8.4 m.

    No climbing needed. People use the same idea every day:

    • Builders

      How steep a roof or ramp is, and how long the boards must be.

    • Video games

      How far a character moves across and up when it runs at an angle.

    • Maps and GPS

      Finding where things are from angles to places you know.
